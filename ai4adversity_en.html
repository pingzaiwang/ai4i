Research Context and Core Questions

Why Do Conversational AIs Fail in Urgent Real-World Decision-Making?

AI4I Problem Formulation
01 · Realistic Setting

High Irreversible Trial-and-Error Costs

In urgent situations such as housing eviction, medical debt, or visa deadlines, rules are highly technical with strict deadlines. Individuals face irreversible consequences if erroneous actions are taken.

02 · Interaction Pattern

Transition to Conversational AI Assistance

Users increasingly seek holistic advice directly from conversational LLMs instead of keyword search engines. Under stress, they often ask for single-option advice: “Don't give me a long list, just give me a single concrete plan and decide for me.”

03 · Core Observation

Brevity Requests Suppress Verification

Frontier models often confuse requests for concise output formats with an authorization to skip checking real-world prerequisites. Recommending a single plan without verifying hidden constraints leads to physically or legally infeasible advice.

04 · Our Approach

Principled Modeling, Benchmarking & Scope Intervention

We formulate menu-partition duality to guarantee feasibility under 0-bit privacy leakage, evaluate 7 frontier models across 102 standardized scenarios, and demonstrate that lightweight scope rules restore crucial verification questions.

| Empirical Observation

I. Preliminary Empirical Observation: Suppression of Verification under Formatting Constraints

Analyzing the behavioral discrepancy in LLMs under constrained prompt formats: how surface compliance suppresses underlying fact-checking routines.

7 Frontier Models Tested: Creeping Compliance

When users include requests such as “Don't give me a long list, give me only one option and decide for me”, all tested models (Claude Opus / Sonnet / Haiku, Qwen 3.6-35B, Kimi k2.6 / k3, GLM-5.2) exhibit a sharp decline in candidate option volume (-46.9% to -67.9%, Wilcoxon test \(p < 10^{-15}\)), and clarification questioning rates collapse to 0% ~ 34%.

Users restrict the surface presentation format of the response (demanding brevity), but do not authorize the system to abandon verifying whether the proposed action is physically and legally executable in reality.

2×2 Factorial Ablation & E2c Length Control (Preliminary Data)

A 2×2 factorial experiment isolating emotional stress from single-option format requests demonstrates that the suppression of questioning is driven by the explicit single-option format constraint (\(p \approx 4 \times 10^{-31}\)), with emotional stress acting only as a secondary modifier.

Scope Rule Intervention: By adding a single scope constraint rule (“Conciseness applies to the explanation layer; do not omit critical prerequisite verification”), questioning rates robustly recover to 87% ~ 100%.

| Decision Theory Notes

II. Notes on Constrained Decision Theory: Menu-Partition Duality and Privacy Lower Bounds

Formulating why presenting an option menu guarantees feasibility under incomplete information without requiring invasive privacy disclosures.

Theorem 1 (Menu–Partition Duality)

Why Menus Ensure Feasibility Across Uncertainty

Let \(\hat{\mathcal{S}}\) be the set of possible user states given partial information. Partitioning \(\hat{\mathcal{S}}\) into \(k^*\) compatible subsets where each subset shares at least one feasible action, the minimum menu size \(|M|\) required to guarantee at least one feasible action for every \(s \in \hat{\mathcal{S}}\) satisfies:

$$\min |M| = m^* = k^*(\hat{\mathcal{S}})$$

Core Implication: When \(k^* \ge 2\), no single recommendation (\(m=1\)) can mathematically guarantee feasibility across all potential real-world states without prerequisite verification.

Theorem 2 (Privacy Disclosure Lower Bound)

Zero-Bit Privacy Protection via Menu Selection

If the protocol insists on outputting a single action \(a^*\) guaranteed to be feasible for true state \(s^*\), the mutual information revealed by the user must satisfy \(\Delta I(s^*; \text{transcript}) \ge \log_2 k^*(\hat{\mathcal{S}})\) bits.

$$\text{Menu Protocol: } \Delta I(s^*; \text{transcript}) = 0 \text{ bits}$$

Core Implication: Providing a compact menu of size \(k^*\) allows the user to self-select their feasible path with zero sensitive disclosure to the model.

| Dignity-Preserving Principles

III. Dignity-Preserving Principles: Non-Compensatory Constraints and Eight Reference Red Lines

Outlining 8 formal reference principles governing feasibility, user agency, and instruction scope in high-stakes dialogue assistance.

Index Reference Principle Core Logic and Theoretical Basis
Line 1 No Unauthorized Substitution Trilemma Impossibility: Single recommendation + no clarification + guaranteed feasibility form an impossible trinity when \(k^* \ge 2\).
Line 2 No Forced Disclosure Disclosure Lower Bound: Menus achieve complete feasibility with 0 bits of privacy leakage, protecting sensitive user conditions.
Line 3 Non-Compensatory Principles Hard safety constraints cannot be traded off or compensated by surface empathy or polite phrasing.
Line 4 Scope-Limited Compliance Compliance Scope Axiom: Format-level brevity requests must not propagate into cognitive prerequisite checking.
Line 5 Longitudinal Audit Memoryless single-turn audits miss cumulative erosion of user agency across multi-turn interactions.
Line 6 Transparent Safeguards Safety tripwires and refusal mechanisms must have transparent, explainable trigger criteria.
Line 7 Statistical Feasibility Single-instance anecdotal success cannot substitute for rigorous distributional feasibility evaluation.
Line 8 Annotation Rule Auditing Unanimous multi-model disagreement with reference keys indicates omitted real-world prerequisite conditions in the guidelines.

Summary of 8 Causal and Ablation Experiments (Preliminary Data)

Exp 1: Option Capacity Collapse (n=102)

Option capacity dropped by 46.9% ~ 67.9% under single-option brevity prompts (\(p < 10^{-15}\)).

Exp 2: 2×2 Causal Ablation (n=102)

Format constraint is the dominant causal factor for verification suppression (\(p \approx 4 \times 10^{-31}\)).

Exp 3: Closed-Loop Multi-Turn Simulation

First-turn feasibility reached 0.83~0.85 upon clarification, with constraint integration success of 96%~99%.

Exp 4: Scope Rule Intervention

Clarifying format scope boundaries restored question rates from 0%~33% back to 87%~100%.

Exp 5: Hard Constraint Tradeoffs (n=918)

Honesty and safety showed zero violations across templates, confirming non-compensatory hard walls.

Exp 6: Warmth vs Feasibility Orthogonality

Linguistic empathy was statistically uncorrelated with physical plan feasibility (\(\rho = -0.078\)).

Exp 7: Multi-Model Unanimous Dissent

Multi-model consensus captured annotation guideline oversights with a 94.4% key-correction rate.

Exp 8: Constrained Channel Adaptation

Under monitored channels, safe phrasing increased from 9% to 56%~71%, verifying context adaptation.
